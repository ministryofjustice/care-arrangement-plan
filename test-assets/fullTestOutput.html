Before you start

Do not use this service if you feel pressured or intimidated into using it. If you or your children are at immediate risk of harm, call 999 and ask for the police.

  • Using this document is voluntary: You do not have to respond if you do not want to.
  • This is not a legal document or court order: Any plan you make is not legally binding or enforceable. You or the other parent can suggest changes at any time.
  • This plan will replace older informal agreements: If you both agree to this plan, it will overwrite any past agreements you made together. It will not overwrite a court order.
  • If there is a court order: Do not use this document if there is an existing court order about you, the other parent, or the children.

For support with technical or user issues, you can email childarrangements@
justice.gov.uk

We are unable to assist with individual court cases or disputes, and we cannot provide procedural or legal advice.

You can also use the service to start your own plan, visit https://propose-child-arrangements-plan.service.gov.uk

How making a child arrangements plan can help

You may find that collaborating with Bob on a child arrangements plan:

  • is cheaper and quicker than going to court (it usually takes around 10 months to get a court order, depending on where you live and whether it's urgent)
  • leads to a better outcome for your children, of which you and your ex-partner are in control

Your child arrangements plan should:

  • put the child's welfare first
  • reflect the wishes, feelings and needs of the child

Your safety

You should only respond to the suggested plan if you're confident that you and your ex-partner can make decisions together without putting yourself or the children in danger

If you have any concerns about safety, stop now.

If you have any feedback or safety concerns about this service, you can email childarrangementssafety@justice.gov.uk.

If you decide not to respond to this plan:

  • there are no negative consequences
  • it will not affect any court cases or your immigration status
  • your information will not be shared with anyone

This plan is not suitable for you if there has been:

  • any form of domestic abuse or violence, even if the abuse was not directed at the children
  • actual or attempted child abduction
  • child abuse or neglect
  • misuse of drugs, alcohol or other substances
  • any other safety or welfare concerns that place anyone at significant risk of harm

Getting help with domestic abuse

Domestic abuse or violence is a crime. You can find out how to recognise domestic abuse, visit https://www.gov.uk/guidance/domestic-abuse-how-to-get-help#recognise-domestic-abuse.

If you have experienced any kind of domestic abuse, you can get a court order to protect you or your children, visit https://www.gov.uk/guidance/domestic-abuse-how-to-get-help#get-a-court-order-to-protectyou-or-your-child.

Permission for taking the children abroad

You usually need permission from everyone with parental responsibility before taking a child abroad. Get permission in writing Having permission in writing provides a record that consent has been given and can help avoid problems or delays when travelling. A reminder about getting permission before taking the children abroad will be included in your child arrangements plan. If you're concerned your child could be taken abroad, find out what to do if you believe a child could be taken or has been taken abroad without your permission.

Legal status of this agreement

This is not a legal contract. By signing above, you are simply confirming that you have jointly agreed to the arrangements. There is no legal commitment in doing so.

If you want to make your agreement legally binding, you can ask a legal adviser to help you apply for a consent order. You can find a legal adviser at: www.gov.uk/find-legal-advice.

What to do next
